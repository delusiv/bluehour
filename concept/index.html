[02] How we work

  1. Signal
  2. Observation
  3. Interpretation
  4. Intervention
  5. Response
01 / Signal

Something small starts showing up.

A new flavour on a konbini shelf at 11pm. A dish a creator keeps coming back to. We log signals across markets, not just headlines.

02 / Observation

Then we go and look.

Markets, shelves, kitchens, homes. Who rushes and who lingers, what people reach for, where they hesitate.

03 / Interpretation

We read it from several angles at once.

Ethnography, psychology and retail, layered until one picture holds: what it means, who it is for, and why it is moving now.

04 / Intervention

We put something into the world.

A tasting, a shelf placement, a reframed brand story. Small, deliberate moves designed to be learned from.

05 / Response

And watch what people actually do.

Picking up, comparing, tasting, putting back. That response is the evidence your next decision rests on.

The People

Two researchers, two disciplines, one team. No handoffs between methods. No handoffs between us.

Jesse Hsu

Food and Sustainability | Design Ethnography

Jesse walks into a shop and notices everything at once. The pace, the mood, the colours, the organisation, the lighting. What feels imported and from where. What’s unique to this place and nowhere else.

Across Tokyo, Brighton, Sydney, and Honolulu, his work asks what makes sustainable food routines the default choice — always asking what happens when good ideas try to scale.

Yimei Chuah

Developmental Psychology | Health & Well-being

Yimei walks into a store and tunes in to the human landscape. Who rushes and who lingers. How people choose products with certainty, hesitation, and care.

A developmental psychology researcher in Auckland, she investigates how children and adults connect with food, the meaning it carries, and its impacts on identity, wellbeing and sustainability.
